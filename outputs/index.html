Click once on the map to set a new start point, click again to set the destination — every strategy recomputes instantly, client-side, against the real terrain grid.

Data: 40×40 elevation grid over Mount Si / North Bend, WA (47.483–47.522°N, 121.748–121.717°W), point-sampled live from the USGS 3DEP Elevation Point Query Service (1 m LIDAR-derived DEM). Basemap tiles © OpenTopoMap (CC-BY-SA), © OpenStreetMap contributors.

All four routers share one terrain-cost engine (8-connected A* over the elevation grid, with line-of-sight post-smoothing) and differ only in how they price each edge: shortest ignores slope entirely (flagging why that's a bad idea for hiking), fastest uses Tobler's hiking-speed function, min‑gain penalizes ascent directly, and safer penalizes steep grades quadratically with a tighter hard cutoff.
